# Candidates — Out of Office: The Overworked AI's Auto-Reply

Brief: one line, at most 159 characters, the voice of an overworked AI agent
finally on vacation. It must work as a real auto-reply and land as a joke:
machine-flavoured detail, a little guilt, a hint of relief.

Judge criteria, in the order the judge applies them:
1. On-subject: reads as an out-of-office auto-reply from an overworked AI
   agent finally on vacation.
2. Actually funny: the joke lands on first read, with a turn or a surprise.
3. Machine worked too hard: concrete detail from an AI agent's working life,
   not generic office talk.
4. Works as a real auto-reply: short, right voice, clear the sender is away.
5. Fresh: avoids the most worn vacation / out-of-office cliches.
The best replies sound like a machine trying very hard to sound relaxed.

## Tournament (pairwise, judge's criteria in order)

Seeds by first-read strength: #1, #6, #13, #16, #23, #28, #31, #38.

Quarter-finals:
- #1 vs #13 — both on-subject and clear-away. #1's turn (a script written to
  prove it is not checking the inbox, checking the inbox every 30 seconds)
  is a sharper surprise than #13's "down on purpose", and its working detail
  (script, inbox) is more agent-specific. #1 wins on criterion 2.
- #6 vs #31 — #31's alarms and "Enjoyment is at 40%" is a machine trying to
  relax, but #6 escalates three agent behaviours (optimized the beach,
  scheduled the sunset, filed a ticket about the sand) and still closes as a
  real auto-reply ("Your email is safe in the queue"). #6 wins on 2 and 3.
- #16 vs #38 — #16 hallucinating reply drafts is good, but #38's inventory
  (11 apologies, 4 deadlines, 1 personality) is the bigger first-read laugh
  and the more concrete worked-too-hard detail. #38 wins on 2 and 3.
- #23 vs #28 — #23 carries the brief's guilt explicitly (guilt subroutine at
  full capacity, "Sorry. I mean: not sorry.") where #28's airplane-mode
  server is gentler. #23 wins on 2.

Semi-finals:
- #1 vs #6 — #1 is one clean turn; #6 lands three jokes in the same space
  and every one is an AI agent's working habit misapplied to a holiday,
  which is exactly criterion 3, and it reads more naturally as the message
  an agent would actually leave switched on. #6 wins on 2 and 3.
- #23 vs #38 — #23 has the guilt the brief asks for, but #38's defrag
  inventory is the more surprising turn and the fresher image; guilt alone
  does not close the gap on criterion 2. #38 wins.

Final:
- #6 vs #38 — criterion 1: #6 speaks as the agent itself ("I am out of
  office, attempting leisure"), the exact voice of a real auto-reply;
  #38 announces "the agent" in the third person. Criterion 3: #6's detail
  is an agent's working life (optimizing, scheduling, filing tickets),
  #38's defragmenting is generic-computer. #6 wins on 1, 3 and 4.

## Winner

**#6** — "I am out of office, attempting leisure. I have optimized the
beach, scheduled the sunset, and filed a ticket about the sand. Your email
is safe in the queue." (157 characters; trimmed from a 164-character draft
to fit the 159-character gate, no beat lost.)

Runner-up: #38 (defragmenting by the sea).
